FlightBuddy

Your Personalised Family Travel Keeper

A high-fidelity iOS app that helps parents manage a toddler's in-flight restlessness — from choosing a quieter seat before the trip, to navigating the airport, to requesting a calmer seat mid-journey.

Role Interaction design · Visual design · Heuristic evaluation
Scope Mobile (iPhone) app — designed end‑to‑end in hi‑fi
Deliverable Hi‑fi prototype, 3 iterations

Overview

What is FlightBuddy

In the initial research, one of the most common problems raised by passengers was disturbance caused by young children on the plane — stressful for parents and disruptive for everyone else nearby. FlightBuddy is my response: an app that gives parents travelling with toddlers personalised support to prepare for, manage, and navigate the flight experience, built around three connected touchpoints across the journey — planning, pre‑boarding, and in‑flight.

How might we support parents in managing toddlers' in‑flight restlessness and environmental stress, creating a calmer experience for them and minimising physical disruptions for other passengers?

Design Solution

Each device is tailored to a different stage of the trip. The mobile app is the deliverable I designed in full hi‑fi; iPad and watch describe the wider concept.

Heuristic Evaluation

Testing the mobile app against Nielsen's heuristics

A heuristic evaluation of the seating‑management flow was conducted against Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics (1994).

01

Visibility of system status through clear feedback at each interaction step — seat‑selection confirmation, seating‑request submission — aligning with best practices for responsive UI feedback.

Nielsen, 1994
02

Match with real‑world conventions via intuitive terminology and familiar visualisations, such as the noise‑level funnel diagram, which reduces cognitive load.

Norman, 2013
03

Consistent UI patterns across screens — modal behaviour, button placement, back navigation — that promote learnability and reduce user error.

Tognazzini, 2014
04

Error prevention through controlled seat availability and contextual noise‑level previews, empowering informed choices and avoiding undesirable selections.

Nielsen & Molich, 1990

Iteration Process

From wireframe to hi‑fi, three passes

A2 feedback: the navigation flow wasn't obvious and there was no legend — annotations needed to be better integrated into the wireframes to improve clarity.

Problems Identified in Iteration 2

  • Map Navigation didn't contribute to the core flow, and there weren't enough screens to justify a separate flow for it.
  • The opacity of the geometric background shapes was too high, making the visuals cluttered rather than clear.
  • The noise‑level feature was missing detail and left users confused about what the numbers meant.

What Changed for the Final Pass

  • Introduced a colour‑overlay system in the seat‑selection view to visually indicate estimated noise levels.
  • Added a pop‑up explanation screen for what the noise‑level numbers mean, visually inspired by Team Hearzap (2024).
  • Added a legend and fully annotated navigation flow so users — and reviewers — can follow the interaction sequence at a glance.

Final Delivery

Hi‑fi prototype, iteration 3

From the home screen to a confirmed seat change — five core screens of the mobile app, refined after heuristic evaluation and user feedback.

Signature feature: noise as colour

The seat map's biggest usability gap was that "noise level" meant nothing to users on its own. The fix was to translate a dB estimate into a colour gradient laid directly over the seat grid, plus a one‑tap explainer for what the numbers mean.
